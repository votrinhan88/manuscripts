\section{Summary of Contributions} \label{sec:61-conclusion-summary}

\begin{foldable}
  The three contributions trace a progression of increasingly tight constraints, each answered by a more principled form of active fidelity enforcement.
  What unifies them is not a shared architecture but a shared design stance: when data is scarce, access is restricted, or tokens are discrete, fidelity to the source distribution must be built in, not assumed.
\end{foldable}

\begin{foldable}
  \textbf{DivBFKD} (Chapter~\ref{ch:30-research01}) addresses the few-shot black-box \ac{KD} problem, where the student has access to only a small labeled image set and may query the teacher solely for its predicted class probabilities.
  The key observation is that diversity of the training distribution---not merely its size---determines whether WGAN-synthesized samples can provide useful gradient signal.
  DivBFKD enforces fidelity by introducing high-confidence images, those the teacher classifies with high certainty, as real-image exemplars in the WGAN discriminator loop on-the-fly.
  Adaptive per-class thresholds keep the easiest classes from dominating, so each class contributes reliably labeled exemplars from well inside the teacher's class regions to anchor the generator.
  An ablation study shows that the adaptive-threshold scheme improves student accuracy over a plain WGAN, and the teacher's class-wise confidence bias motivates per-class rather than global thresholds.
  DivBFKD achieves the highest accuracy among the compared few-shot black-box \ac{KD} methods (BBKD and FS-BBT) across the image classification datasets and architectures evaluated.
\end{foldable}

\begin{foldable}
  \textbf{DIPKD} (Chapter~\ref{ch:40-research02}) addresses the extreme case of access restriction: no real data at all, and a teacher that returns only top-1 labels.
  The fidelity problem becomes entirely generative---the student must be trained on a synthetic distribution that aims to cover the inputs relevant to the teacher's decisions, without any real-image anchor.
  DIPKD addresses this through a three-phase pipeline where each phase serves a distinct purpose.
  The Synthesis phase constructs hierarchical multi-scale image priors from uniform noise with nonlinear filters and semantic cutmixing, whose masks are irregular regions rather than object segments, producing structural diversity before any teacher query.
  The Contrast phase passes these priors through a primer student backbone and applies an instance-level contrastive objective that optimizes the synthetic dataset directly, pushing each synthetic image away from all others in embedding space to prevent mode collapse.
  The Distillation phase then takes soft outputs from the primer student as surrogate soft supervision, in place of the dark knowledge the black-box teacher withholds, and uses them alongside hard teacher labels to train the final student.
  DIPKD achieves state-of-the-art performance on 11 of the 12 benchmarks (all except Tiny-ImageNet), with substantial gains on several complex and domain-specific datasets where synthetic diversity is most constrained.
\end{foldable}

\begin{foldable}
  \textbf{TAKE} (Chapter~\ref{ch:50-research03}) shifts the problem domain from image model compression to text dataset distillation~(\ac{DD}), and with it the distributional fidelity problem shifts from generation to selection.
  The goal is to replace a large labeled text corpus with a compact surrogate that approximates downstream task performance.
  TAKE is the first text \ac{DD} method to simultaneously satisfy three desiderata: knowledge-based weighting of samples by their task-aligned contribution, a dataset-level distillation objective that aligns the selected corpus with the full source distribution, and human-readable output that is auditable and transferable across architectures.
  Trajectory-aware influence scoring computes importance weights from reciprocal gradient norms accumulated across the training trajectory, correcting the hard-sample bias, whereby scoring at the convergence point tends to assign inflated importance to hard examples such as noisy or boundary ones.
  An \ac{LLM} generation pool produces diverse textual candidates, and discrete \ac{OT} via the Sinkhorn algorithm selects the subset that globally minimizes the entropically regularized transport cost between the candidate pool and the source distribution.
  The importance-weighted \ac{OT} objective combines task alignment and distributional coverage as complementary objectives, and in our ablation, adding importance weights to the \ac{OT} objective improves over \ac{OT} alone.
  TAKE matches or exceeds prior text \ac{DD} baselines across six language benchmarks at extreme compression, establishing the first end-to-end text \ac{DD} method with theoretical guarantees.
\end{foldable}

\begin{foldable}
  Across all three contributions, the fidelity-first design stance opens the potential for deployment in regimes that scale-dependent assumptions foreclose: edge and embedded devices for DivBFKD and DIPKD, and low-resource, privacy-constrained, and carbon-sensitive language model development for TAKE.
  Model compression reduces inference cost and latency; dataset distillation reduces training expenditure, data-governance risk, and cumulative carbon footprint across full development lifecycles.
  The practical impact scales with the tightness of the constraint: the more restricted the access regime, the larger the relative gain from enforcing fidelity actively rather than assuming it.
\end{foldable}
